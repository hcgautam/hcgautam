Most of the argument about air pollution in India happens downstream of numbers that very few people have looked at closely. A city gets an AQI. A report says life expectancy falls by some number of years. A sensor on a rooftop disagrees with the regulatory monitor two kilometres away. All of it is defensible, and all of it is easy to misread.

This is where I'll write about that middle layer: how air quality data is produced, where it breaks, and what it can honestly support.

Topics I expect to return to:

- **Satellite PM<sub>2.5</sub>** — what a gridded estimate actually measures, and how far it can be pushed.
- **Population-weighted exposure** — why the average concentration over a district is not the average concentration a person breathes.
- **Emission inventories** — the least glamorous and most decisive input to any clean-air plan.
- **Low-cost sensors** — what they are good for once you stop pretending they are reference monitors.
- **Machine learning** — where it beats a well-specified statistical model, and where it just hides the assumptions.
- **AQI design** — a single number that has to be honest, actionable and understandable at the same time.

The aim is to make technical air quality work easier to understand, reproduce and use.
