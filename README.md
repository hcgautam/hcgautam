## About me

I am an environmental engineer working on the measurement, modelling and communication of air pollution. My work sits where messy environmental data meets the decisions that have to be made from it — satellite PM<sub>2.5</sub>, regulatory monitoring, low-cost sensors, emission inventories, meteorology, population exposure and life expectancy.

At **EPIC** I run the **Air Quality Life Index (AQLI)** analysis and reporting pipeline: processing satellite-derived PM<sub>2.5</sub>, calculating population-weighted concentrations, analysing 25 years of GADM Level-2 air quality and life-expectancy data, and building the visualisations and reproducible workflows behind the annual AQLI report — a number that ends up in policy debate and on front pages, so the chain of assumptions behind it has to be defensible all the way down.

Before EPIC, I spent nearly four years at **CSTEP, Bengaluru**, building comprehensive emission inventories for **76 Indian cities**, contributing to state and city clean-air action plans, validating low-cost sensor networks, and modelling satellite-derived PM<sub>2.5</sub>. Earlier, at **IIT Madras**, I worked on vehicular emissions in Delhi (CADTIME, under the NERC–MoES APHH programme) and on air quality management for the Jawaharlal Nehru Port Trust region.

My **Ph.D.** (IIT Madras, Environmental Engineering) developed neuro-fuzzy approaches to urban air quality management: statistical trend analysis of criteria pollutants, extreme value theory for predicting NAAQS violations, ANN-based forecasting for Chennai, and a fuzzy-logic AQI designed to give real-time health advisories that people could actually act on.

I care about work that is reproducible and legible to the people who have to use it: version-controlled analysis, transparent assumptions about where numbers come from, and charts a journalist or a pollution control board officer can read without a methods section.
